# Interview sprint

Most assignments are lost to sequencing, not to ability. Candidates over-research, decide
late, and build a prototype that arrives without an argument. This skill fixes the order
and enforces gates.

## Opening move

Establish in two minutes, then start:
- The deliverable and its format, exactly as the brief states it. Read the brief twice and
  quote the deliverable back. Answering a different question than the one asked is the most
  common cause of rejection and it is entirely avoidable.
- Hard deadline and realistic working hours inside it.
- The evaluation criteria, stated or inferred from the role and company.
- Whether a prototype is required, optional, or would be a differentiator.

Then produce the plan with time boxes and gates, and get agreement before starting work.

## The 24-hour shape (compress or expand proportionally)

| Block | Hours | Output | Gate before moving on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | 0.5 | Restated question, audience, evaluation criteria, deliverable list | One sentence stating what "good" means here |
| Research | 2 | Fact base with sources, market context, competitor moves, any public numbers | Stop on time regardless of completeness. Note what is unknown |
| Diagnose | 1.5 | Objective, binding constraint, product's job; segment and job chosen | The arc rule holds and is written down |
| Decide | 1.5 | Options generated and scored, one direction chosen, refusals recorded | The decision is written before any building starts |
| Economics and metrics | 1.5 | Contribution logic, north star, tree, guardrails, decision rule | The numbers are consistent with the segment size |
| Build | 5 | Prototype core loop, deployed, unhappy path present | Deployed link exists before styling begins |
| Artefacts | 2 | Diagrams, model, deck or document per the brief | Every artefact has a caption and an assumptions table |
| Assemble | 2 | The deliverable in the required format, answer first | A stranger could follow it without you |
| Red team | 1.5 | Critique and fixes | Score at or above 3 on every rubric dimension |
| Rehearse | 1.5 | Timed walkthrough, twice, plus the appendix for expected questions | Comes in under time with 20% spare |
| Buffer | 5 | Sleep, overruns, deployment failures | Never plan into this block |

Adaptations:
- **Four hours**: frame 15 min, research 30, diagnose 30, decide 30, metrics 30,
  artefacts 45, assemble 45, red team 15. No prototype unless one is explicitly required;
  a wireframe and a flow diagram carry the argument instead.
- **One week**: keep the same order, add a real user conversation in the research block and
  a second red-team pass by a different person 24 hours before submission.

## Non-negotiable gates

1. **Decide before you build.** No code until the direction is written down. A prototype
   built during the deciding phase becomes the decision, which is how people end up
   defending a feature they never chose.
2. **Deploy at the halfway mark.** A prototype that is not deployed by the deadline does not
   exist. Deploy a skeleton early and push improvements.
3. **Answer first.** The deliverable opens with the recommendation, then the support. If it
   opens with methodology, restructure it.
4. **Cut to the time limit before rehearsing**, not during. Cutting live wrecks the arc.
5. **Never plan into the buffer.** Deployment failures and a laptop update will consume it.

## Parallelisation

Where subagents or parallel tasks are available, run research and fact-gathering in
parallel across three or four threads (market, competitors, the product itself, public
numbers) while the diagnosis begins on what has already returned. Do not parallelise
deciding; that is one thread and it is the user's.

## Working with the user

- Report at every gate in two lines: what is done, what is next, what is at risk.
- If the schedule slips, cut scope in this order: artefact polish, prototype styling, extra
  analysis depth, prototype screens. Never cut the red team or the rehearsal.
- Keep a running list of what was cut, because "what else would you have done" is a
  standard question and a prepared list is a strong answer.

## Output

The plan, then the deliverables, then a rehearsal script with the four questions most likely
to be asked and a prepared answer for each.
