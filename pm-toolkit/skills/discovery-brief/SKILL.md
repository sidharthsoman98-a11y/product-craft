# Discovery brief

Discovery separates what is known from what is believed, then buys the cheapest evidence
that would change a decision. Research that cannot change a decision is not worth running.

## Procedure

1. **Assumption inventory.** List every assumption the idea rests on, split into: desirability
   (do they want it), viability (does the money work), feasibility (can it be built),
   usability (can they operate it), and ethics (who is harmed). Rank by two axes, how
   uncertain and how fatal if wrong. Only the top-right quadrant is worth researching.
2. **Turn the top assumptions into falsifiable statements.** "Users will switch payment
   method to save two rupees" is testable. "Users want convenience" is not.
3. **Choose the method that fits the question**, and name its weakness:
   - Behaviour questions: instrumentation, log analysis, or an observed task, never a survey.
   - Motivation questions: switch interviews, five to eight, and stop when the stories repeat.
   - Preference at scale: survey, but only after qualitative work has fixed the wording.
   - Willingness to pay: a real commitment proxy, a pre-order or a price ladder, never a
     stated-intent question.
   - Feasibility: a spike or a conversation with an engineer, not a PM's guess.
4. **Write the interview guide** if interviews are the method: open with the last time it
   happened, walk the actual episode chronologically, ask what they did rather than what
   they would do, ask about the workaround, and end with what they gave up to switch. Never
   pitch the solution inside the interview.
5. **Synthesise existing material** if the user has notes, tickets, reviews or transcripts:
   tag each observation with segment, job, force (push, pull, habit, anxiety), and severity.
   Cluster by job, not by feature request. Count how many independent sources support each
   theme, and keep the count visible, because a theme from one loud user is a hypothesis,
   not a finding.
6. **Evidence ledger.** A table of every claim with its evidence class: measured,
   observed, reported, inferred, assumed. This ledger travels with the idea into the PRD
   and is the honest core of the whole method.
7. **Decide.** State what the evidence supports, what it does not, and the single next test.

## Synthetic panels

When no users are available, a simulated panel is permissible for pressure-testing only.
Construct three to five distinct segment personas with specific circumstances, put the
concept to each, and record objections. Label the entire output as synthetic. It generates
hypotheses and finds obvious flaws; it is never evidence of demand, and saying so
unprompted is a credibility marker.

## Output

Problem statement, segment and job, evidence ledger, top assumptions with tests, research
plan with method and sample and timeline, synthesis if material exists, and the decision
the research will enable.
